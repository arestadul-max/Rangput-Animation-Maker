Rongpat Studio

Your offline cartoon maker. Write the story in a spreadsheet, add your voice recordings, double-click, and get a finished YouTube Short in the Rongpat style. No internet, no AI tokens.

Quick start

  1. Unzip the whole RongpatStudio folder somewhere simple, for example C:\RongpatStudio or your Desktop. Keep all the folders together.
  2. Try the demo first: double-click 2 - Preview Video (fast), type the number of Demo - The Last Ball, press Enter twice. In a minute or two the video opens.
    The first time, Windows may say “Windows protected your PC”. Click More info → Run anyway. This happens because the files are new, not because they are unsafe.
  3. Double-click 1 - New Project and type a name for your story. A folder opens with scenes.xlsx and empty voice, images and sounds folders.
  4. Record your lines and put them in the voice folder (wav, mp3 or m4a, any names, e.g. 01_mishti.wav).
  5. Open scenes.xlsx and write one row per shot (see below). The row under the headings explains every column, and the dropdowns help you pick places and moods. Save and close Excel.
  6. Double-click 2 - Preview Video (fast) to check it, fix rows, and preview again. When it’s right, double-click 3 - Make Final Video for the full HD 1080×1920 file. Videos are saved in your project’s output folder.

Speed: a 60-second preview takes about 1–3 minutes on a normal laptop; the final video about 5–15 minutes (more CPU cores = faster).

Characters, places, actions, sounds

Every scenes.xlsx has tabs at the bottom listing everything you can type: Characters, Places, Actions (with emotes and cameras) and Sounds (effects and music moods). The pictures Characters - names to type.png and Places - names to type.png in the main folder show them all.

Mishti, Mechho and Tutul are fully animated puppets: Mishti sits, runs, leaps, meows and hisses; Mechho floats, screams, cries, glows and holds a fish or a sweet; Tutul talks, smiles and offers a sweet. All other characters blink, talk (their mouths move with the voice), walk, jump, shake, bounce, spin, faint, sleep, glow, appear and vanish.

Walking and running (walk-to, run-to, enter-left …) now move the legs and swing the arms. Things a character already holds in the bible (a phone, a kettle, a ruler) move with their hand.

Objects

Built in: ball, bat, stumps. Put an object in the Cast like a character (stumps right back) and give it actions:

ball: in-hand mitul, bowl mitul pupu @1.2, hit-up @1.9 umbrella: in-hand haridas, throw haridas tuli @1.0, in-hand tuli left @1.7 kite: fly-in pupu @0.5 sign: swing coin: spin bag: drop @2

Object actions: in-hand (add left/right), bowl, throw, hit-up, hit-left, hit-right, fly-in, drop, spin, bounce, swing, appear, vanish. Camera ball follows the ball (or the focus object).

Your own objects: drop an .svg or transparent .png into library\props. The file name is its name. Optional .json: {"name": "Umbrella", "size": "large", "grip": "bottom"} (size: tiny, small, medium, large, huge or a number; grip: where a hand holds it: top, center, bottom).

Expressions

Faces change by themselves: from the line being said (“!”, “?”, “…” and how loud), from what the character is doing, and from emotes. Eyes look at whoever is speaking, and listeners react. To choose one yourself, use the Expression column (the last column): pupu: angry @1.2, mitul: smug.

Expressions: neutral, happy, excited, laughing, sad, crying, angry, scared, surprised, shocked, confused, thinking, nervous, embarrassed, smug, sleepy, disgusted, love, stern.

Cricket matches (update 1.3)

Use the place cricket-pitch for real batting and bowling, filmed down the pitch like cricket on TV. The View column picks the camera:

ViewWhat you see
battingThe batter faces us at the crease, stumps and keeper behind, fielders around. The ball comes in, bounces, and is hit or goes to the keeper.
bowlingThe bowler runs in towards us, jumps and bowls; the ball comes at the camera (or flies back over the bowler after a big hit).

You only write who bowls to whom and when. Everything else is automatic: the batter takes guard and swings at the right moment, the bowler runs in and bowls, the keeper crouches, everyone else becomes a fielder. The Cast can stay empty (it uses whoever was on screen before).

Place: cricket-pitch · View: bowling · Actions: ball: bowl mitul pupu @1.6 Place: cricket-pitch · View: batting · Actions: babai: keeper; ball: bowl mitul pupu @0.05, hit-up @0.75

After the ball: hit-up (big hit into the sky), hit-left / hit-right (along the ground), or nothing (the keeper catches it). A good order is: batting view (the batter gets ready), bowling view (the run-up), batting view (the hit).

Looks and sound (update 1.3)

  • Natural legs: characters stand with straight legs and planted feet; knees only bend outward in a real squat (keeper, crouch), never inward.
  • Acting with the body: while talking, characters use changing hand gestures (open palm, both hands out, hand on chest), lean in and nod; when quiet they shift their weight instead of standing frozen.
  • Open, living eyes: every face with eyes can blink, look around and change expression, including characters drawn with shut eyes (Babai, Baba, Ghonta-da).
  • Marks stay on the head: sweat drops, anger marks, “!”, “?”, hearts and shock lines are placed and sized from each character’s head, so they never float away.
  • Film-style camera: close frames the face and shoulders, medium frames the people talking from the knees up. Captions move to the bottom by themselves if they would cover a face.
  • Cinematic light: a colour grade for day, evening and night, a soft key light on whoever the shot is about, gentle haze behind the characters and a soft-focus background in close-ups (final video only; the fast preview skips the blur).
  • Balanced sound: music sits about 15 dB under the voices and drops another 10 dB while someone speaks, coming back slowly after the line; effects sit under the voices too. The final video is set to YouTube loudness (−14 LUFS).

Automatic rigging for new characters

What you addWhat the app does automatically
A character made with Claude (update pack)Full rig: arms, legs, held objects, expressions. Unzip the pack into library and run 5 - Update Library.
Your own drawing in parts (SVG with groups named head, body, arm-left, arm-right, leg-left, leg-right)Full rig: finds the shoulders, hips and neck; arms do every move, legs walk, head nods and talks, hands hold objects.
A single flat picture (PNG or SVG)Simple rig: head nods, tilts and talks; the body breathes, leans and bounces. For moving arms, draw it in parts.
An object (PNG or SVG in library\props)Can be held, thrown, dropped, spun, swung, bounced or placed.

How to draw in parts (Inkscape, free): draw the character standing, facing you. Select the arm shapes → Object → Group (Ctrl+G) → Object → Object Properties → set ID to arm-left → Set. Do the same for arm-right, leg-left, leg-right, head and body. Draw arms hanging straight down from the shoulder. Save as Plain SVG into library\characters.

Fixing problems

  • Check one shot quickly: double-click 4 - Check One Shot (picture), pick the project, type the shot number. You get a picture in seconds.
  • Preview only some shots: in 2 - Preview Video, type e.g. 3-5 when it asks “Which shots?”.
  • Something is in the wrong place: change the position (left → -60), the View, or the Camera, then check again.
  • Timing is off: add @seconds to actions, emotes and sounds, or set a Duration.
  • A red message appears: it tells you the row and what’s wrong (for example an unknown name, with suggestions). Fix that cell and run again.
  • “Permission denied” on scenes.xlsx: close it in Excel first.
The app can’t judge how a video looks: it does exactly what the sheet says. For brand-new drawings, poses or features, ask Claude, and you’ll get a library update pack or an engine update.

Changing or updating the bible

The cast and places are not locked inside the program. They live in libraries, so you can grow or change the bible any time:

You want to…Do this
Add your own characterPut a standing, full-body .svg or transparent .png in library\characters. The file name is the name you type. Optional .json with the same name: {"name": "Dadu", "bn": "দাদু", "size": "adult"} (sizes: adult, tall, kid, small-kid, big-animal, animal, small-animal, or a number). Then run 5 - Update Library.
Add your own placePut an .svg, .png or .jpg (landscape works best) in library\places. Optional .json: {"name": "Dadu’s shop", "floor": 0.88}. Then run 5 - Update Library.
Use a changed biblePut the changed bible board files (.dc.html/.html) in library\bible-import and run 5 - Update Library. Characters and places with the same names are replaced; new ones are added; blinking and lip-sync are set up automatically.
Get an update from ClaudeAsk for a “Rongpat Studio library update pack”. Unzip it into the library folder and run 5 - Update Library.
Undo a changeDelete the file from library and run 5 - Update Library. The built-in version comes back.

5 - Update Library also refreshes the Characters and Places lists inside all your projects’ scenes.xlsx (close Excel first).

Help

  • Nothing happens / window closes: make sure the python, engine and library folders are next to the .bat files.
  • Antivirus blocks it: allow the RongpatStudio folder. It contains Python, ffmpeg (video maker) and resvg (drawing), all open-source.
  • Very slow: use previews while working; close other big programs; the final video uses all but one CPU core.
  • The folder layout: engine = the program, library = your additions, projects = your stories, python = the built-in Python (don’t change).
